AI Tool Guidance

Artificial intelligence tools—software that reproduces text, images, computer code, audio, video, and other content—have become widely available. Well-known examples include ChatGPT for text and DALL•E for images. This statement governs all such tools, including those released during your enrolled session. 

It is your responsibility—not the tool’s—to assure the quality, integrity, and accuracy of work you submit in all CU Boulder courses. Keep in mind that AI tools are designed to create content that seems to have been produced by a human, not to produce accurate or reliable content; therefore, relying on an AI tool may result in inaccurate content. Be wary of unintentional plagiarism or data fabrication. Act with integrity, for the sake of both your personal character and your academic record.

Consider the impact of using AI tools on your learning goals: in most cases, using an AI-generated answer without critical review and significant input from your end is contrary to the overall goal of personal learning and development in your chosen area.

Respect copyright and privacy. You may only enter your own data into AI tools. Do not enter private, sensitive, or copyrighted data from others into AI tools without their consent. Avoid using AI for purposes that are private, sensitive, or high-risk.

Consider your environmental impact. Using AI models requires large amounts of electricity, water, and rare earth minerals. When using AI, keep in mind there are environmental concerns associated with its use.

You may use AI tools on assignments in this course as noted below.

Full AI Tool Usage

You may use AI tools in this course. Some assignments will require the use of such tools. However, keep in mind your own and others' privacy implications and security risks when using AI tools. If you use AI tools on assignments in CU Boulder courses hosted on Coursera, document your usage per the AI Usage Documentation Best Practices.

AI tools may not be used on the final exam.

AI Usage Documentation Best Practices

When using AI tools in your academic work, it is essential to accurately document how and why you used them. AI tools should be considered as aids rather than primary sources. Therefore, remember to:

  • Reference AI assistance. In your references or bibliography section, mention the AI tool used and the nature of its contribution. For example: "Assistance provided by [AI Tool Name] for generating initial content ideas, response to [prompt], [month, day, year]”.

  • Document your process. Save screenshots, dialog, prompt, queries or version history to document your process if you used AI. Include documentation in the submission if the assignment format allows. Otherwise, save it in your system in case you are asked by a program or university staff. 

Suspected Violations of this Policy

If program staff suspects you may have used AI tools to complete assignments in ways not explicitly authorized or suspect other violations of the honor code, they will contact you via email. Be sure to respond promptly to any related communication so your perspective is included in the case review. Failure to respond timely will not prevent the completion of a case review.

In suspected cases of unauthorized AI tool usage, the program may:

  • Request the documentation noted above (see AI Usage Documentation Best Practices) or other supplementary materials

  • Issue a warning

  • Assign a 0–50% grade for the question

  • Assign a 0–50% grade for the assignment

  • Assign an F grade for the course 

  • Reference prior violations

  • Remove access to the course, related materials, and tools

  • Contact the Office of Student Conduct & Conflict Resolution to report suspected Honor Code violations

Turnitin and similar AI detection tools may be used in these courses for initial detection of possible honor code violations. All suspected violations will be reviewed by a human. AI tools alone will not be used to determine if an assignment is plagiarized, and results from these tools will not be used alone as evidence to penalize students.

Non-credit learners are strongly encouraged to follow the guidelines above. If a non-credit learner upgrades to for-credit, they will be subjected to the above requirements.
