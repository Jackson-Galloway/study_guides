This video marks the start of the Link Layer Module in the Networking
Fundamentals course. To overview, what
we'll be covering in this module will highlight the main problems addressed
at the link layer. To start, let's consider
what a link is. In networks,
communication happens over some physical medium. This can be wires or
can happen wirelessly. The link layer puts some
capabilities on top of that, to not just allow
for transmitting signals over the wires
or through the air, but to enable
communication to occur. This functionality is typically implemented in the network
interface card, or NIC. The NIC is the component
in your computer which the wire is plugged into or has an antenna to be able
to transmit wirelessly. Through a combination of
hardware and firmware on the NIC and some functionality
in the operating system, the link layer enables
communication. The first problem that we'll
cover is how to encode data. We know we can send signals
over a physical medium, but how do you
structure the series of ones and zeros to make it know who the message is destined for and what
the data actually means? The second problem
to address is that the physical medium isn't perfect and these signals
could get corrupted. That is, errors can occur, bits can get flipped. The link layer can allow us
to at least detect that there was an error and perhaps
even correct the error. Some of the physical mediums that could be used to
communicate are not just point-to-point links
between two devices but may be shared among several. You could think of a
Wi-Fi network for this. The key problem to address here is how to regulate access to the physical medium to allow all to be able to
share it effectively. The last problem
we'll discuss in this module is extending to a local area network that
allows communication to extend beyond the limits of
the transmission medium. Thank you. Next time
we'll get into encoding the series of ones and
zeros into frames.
